## Area

wap/kernel: its forms (kernel/forms.wap, kernel/surface.wap) and its leaves, the primitives (core/emit/primitives.wap,
host.wap, opcodes.wap, and the `prim` bodies in wap/types/). Also read: what landed from 2026-11-22 to 2026-11-25.

## Hacks

1. **A statement marked by a Prim.** sugar/calls.wap:15 wraps a script's last statement in `Prim(shown, …)`.
   core/emit/emitter.wap:68 splits the program by that name, and core/emit/primitives.wap:72 emits the Prim as its
   own argument. This is a mark carried in the code's shape ("Facts live in Meta, never in the shape",
   notes/backbone.md), and a Prim written outside the types module (kernel/forms.wap:40-41). Proposal: the rule's
   template carries an annotation (core/rules/templates.wap:22-29 carries them already), and the emitter reads that
   fact. Cost: a few lines, and one special name less.
2. **Typing talks to `format` through a global.** core/analyze/calls.wap:76 declares `typed_readings`, a module
   global that typing fills (:46) and core/format.wap:21 reads. wap/compiler.wap:92 types a program only to fill it,
   and drops the result and any refusal. Proposal: typing gives its readings back with its result, and `formatted`
   passes them to format as an argument. Cost: about 5 lines changed.
3. **Converting to an optional swallows the value's own raise.** sugar/types.wap:6 writes
   `try ($value as $type) else ø`, which also catches a raise from `$value` itself: `read(path) as int?` is ø when
   the file is missing. Proposal: `{ given = $value; converted: $type? = try (given as $type) else ø; converted }`.
   Cost: 1 line.
4. **`optimize` guesses callers from text.** wap/compiler.wap:107-113 (`mentioned_beside`, `names_in`) keeps a
   function when its name appears anywhere in a sibling .wap file, comments included. Proposal: the callers are the
   modules that `use` the file, typed the way `uncalled` (:99) types one. Cost: about 5 lines.
5. **A C call used as a cast.** wap/extensions/native/c.wap:19 `host_of(p) := memmove(p, p, 0)` turns a memory
   address into a host pointer through a C call that hands back its destination. Proposal: the glue's address
   translation as a foreign word of its own. Cost: about 3 lines and one glue function.

## Edge cases to drop

1. **Bools as numbers: drop the rule, or make it one conversion.** wiki/number.md:102-103 says a bool acts as its
   number in any operator (`yes * 3` is 3). The two ways to settle it:
   - **Drop:** remove the sentence, the two overloads, the five test lines and the passages.
   - **Keep:** one declared conversion from bool to int, never an overload per operator.

   The evidence:
   - The compiler refuses `yes * 3` (probed: "no * takes (bool, int)"), and gives the rule to `==` alone, through two
     overloads (wap/types/prelude.wap:231-232).
   - The rule is pinned by wiki/bool.md:5-6, wiki/equality.md:33 and 59, and five test lines (`0 == no`:
     test/test_types.wap:24-26, test/test_bool_page.wap:4, test/test_equality_page.wap:5).
   - A bool in arithmetic is the user's own example of an edge case (AGENTS.md, `"x" * yes`).
   - coc-plan 4 joins unlike types into their union, which would make `0 == no` give no.

   This rewrites the spec, so it goes to the interviewer with the old and new text. Cost of dropping: −2 types
   lines, −5 test lines and four wiki passages.

## Rewrites

### R1. One reading of each word: the theory and the typer

Two typers read every program:
- **core/analyze** (3,491 lines) types what runs;
- **wap/extensions/theory/elaborate** (3,270 lines) types what the theory's kernel checks. It comes after the same
  reader, operators, shape rules and lowering (elaborate/file.wap:5-14), and it resolves names, chooses overloads
  and reads literals by rules of its own.

The census counts what the theory accepts (a floor of 2,168 statements, test/wap/ratchet_census.sh:21), not whether
the two typers agree. They do not: `x := 0 - 3; x` prints -3 when compiled and 0 when run from its terms.

**Verdict: no full rewrite. Finish the switch, in another order.** The kernel (K1 to K7) and the elaborator follow
the plan and share the compiler's front end, so a rewrite would only write them again. The path needs changing:
0. **First, a gate of agreement.** S2's own measure is that every test program prints the same both ways (plan 11,
   S2; `both_ways` in test/wap/scripted.wap). Run it over all test programs as a ratchet, before more theory features
   land. It catches every item below that changes a value.
1. **Make the shared words mean the same** before S3 merges the preludes: the numeral (R1.3), `int` (R1.1), and bools
   as numbers (Edge cases 1). These are meaning questions, for the interviewer. The merge itself stays at S3 (plan
   5.2): the seed rule binds wap/types, and core/analyze types no recursor, level or law.
2. **A host operation is a `prim`.** `runs(…)` keeps pairing a theory constant with what it runs by, but its run
   body becomes a primitive of the one table (More general 1) instead of a wap spelling. That leaves no spelling
   language, no checker and no typer for layouts (R1.2). It also settles the branch theory-host-layouts-2, which
   moves the spelling check onto core/analyze (491763c9): hold that switch, since primitives need no typer. That is
   the Supervisor's call.
3. **S4 before S3.** S2 cannot print the same both ways without the layouts, so the plan's "S4 follows S3" cannot
   hold: the layouts are S2's prerequisite.
4. **S2 into the kernel forms, as a migration step.** This is a plan change, for the interviewer. Lower run terms
   into the forms today's emitter reads, with each node's layout attached by erase from its kernel type
   (`host_type`). Lowering must never infer a layout, or it becomes the old typer again. The plan's emitter for
   erased terms (coc-plan 4, 5.2) follows at S3. A spike of three programs (a closure, a loop, a Try) gives its size
   before anyone counts lines.
5. **S3.** core/analyze goes once the census accepts every statement and the gate holds for every test program.

**R1.1 Two definitions of one word** (split; S3 merges the preludes, plan 5.2).
- **Now:** extensions/theory/prelude/operators.wap:6-38 declares `+ - * / % == <= >= < >` for nat and int, `#` (a
  nat), `+` for lists and texts, `not` and `implies`. wap/types declares the same words (types/prelude.wap:39, 56,
  70, 74, 139; text_bytes.wap:15; lists.wap:39) with other meanings. Take `int`:
  - In the theory and in the user's decision (coc-plan 13) it is the whole numbers.
  - In types/ and in wiki/number.md:100 ("`x: int` … wrap") it is the wrapping i64.
  - `runs(int, int)` (prelude/numbers.wap:176) runs the theory's `int` as the wrapping one, which notes/theory.md
    "The run trust base" admits: "one past 2^63 wraps where the theory's does not".
- **Before S3:** one meaning per shared word. For `int` that means the decision: an overflow test on the fast path,
  and a slow path of big numbers (lib/magnitude.wap). That brings big numbers back into every build, the compiler's
  included, a cost the interviewer weighs with the decision. wiki/number.md then follows the decision.
- **At S3:**
  - each word is defined once in types/, its run body a primitive (R1.2);
  - the trust base is the prelude's `runs(…)` statements, listed by a command instead of the hand table in
    notes/theory.md.
- **Unblocks:** a law speaks about the program's own `+`.

**R1.2 Host operations spelled in wap** (split).
- **Now:**
  - 21 `runs(…)` statements (prelude/numbers.wap 14, logic.wap 4, effects.wap 3) each give a wap spelling, e.g.
    `runs(add, m + n)` or `runs(nat.successor, n + 1, when: it > 0, n: it - 1)`. elaborate/file.wap:83-116 notes
    them.
  - run/erase.wap:245-287 checks each spelling by elaborating it over the host's types in a copied theory.
  - run/lower.wap writes the erased program as wap text, and today's compiler types that text again.
  - On theory-host-layouts-2 the check itself moves to core/analyze (491763c9), because the theory's words take nats
    where the host's take ints (R1.3).
  - At S3 the printed text has no typer left.
- **Proposal:**
  - The run body is a primitive of the one table, e.g. `runs(add, prim i64.add(m, n))`, checked against its row's
    wasm types.
  - A type's layout stays a row: nat runs as an int (plan 9.7, "the same table").
  - The spelling language and its checker go.
- **Lines:** about −80 (erase.wap:245-287, and file.wap:83-116's formatting and placing), plus the rows (More
  general 1).
- **Unblocks:** S2 and S3 without a typer for layouts.

**R1.3 Three readings of a numeral** (split; for the interviewer).
- **The three readings:**
  - core/analyze/types.wap:254 reads a whole numeral as `int`, the wrapping i64.
  - elaborate/read.wap:210-216 reads it as `nat` whatever is wanted, and converts it to an int
    (prelude/conversions.wap:9) only where an int is wanted. Subtraction on nats stops at 0.
  - wiki/number.md:97-100 shows a whole value as `nat`, exact, growing into a big number, and says `x: int` wraps.
- **Probe:** with the doors built from the theory-host-layouts-2 tree, `x := 0 - 3; x` gives:
  - the elaborator's door: `type of x` is `nat`;
  - the run door: it prints 0;
  - the compiled program: it prints -3.
- **Counts too:** `#xs` is a nat in the theory (prelude/operators.wap:27), so `#xs - 1` differs whatever the
  numeral's default.
- **Settled:** `0 - 3` is -3. Printing 0 is the plausible wrong value the rules call the worst bug.
- **Open, for the interviewer:** the default type's name and meaning (int, or an exact whole number as number.md
  says), and what the subtraction of two counts gives.
- **With one elaborator:** one bidirectional rule. A numeral has the type its place wants, else the default. About
  10 lines in read.wap.

**R1.4 Numerals as successors, and the depth budget** (not a split; keep).
- compute.wap:62-74 and numbers.wap:248-254 open a numeral one successor at a time where a recursor meets it.
  environment.wap:9-12 holds the step and depth budgets.
- Numerals are already unbounded host numbers with seven shortcuts (plan 6.7), which is Lean's scheme. Lean's kernel
  also opens a literal one successor at a time under a recursor, and guards the same deep recursion by checking its
  stack and its work.
- A single elaborator would change nothing here.

**R1.5 Whole-theory copies** (split; the census needs them). scripts/theory-census.wap:77-82 copies the whole Theory
before each statement and holds it again on a refusal (elaborate/read.wap:106-134). Keeping a statement's writes
apart and merging them on acceptance would do the same, as Lean's `addDecl` gives back a new environment or a
refusal. Not measured; it goes with the census at S3.

**R1.6 Four doors** (split; at S3 they become the compiler's commands).
- **Now:**
  - check.wap:17 runs its own loop.
  - elaborate/door.wap:10, run/door.wap:14 and elaborate/verify.wap:38 share `door_elaborated`
    (elaborate/file.wap:149).
  - A program's `runs(…)` is refused in two places: bindings.wap:39 and elaborate/declare.wap:58.
- **Proposal:** one door whose modes (check, verify, run, census) follow one reading of the prelude, so the refusal
  is written once. About −20 lines.

**R1.7 Overloads chosen twice** (split; S3 deletes core's chooser).
- **Now:** core/analyze/calls.wap:275-343 ranks candidates by weights (`rank`: typing weight, written structure,
  derived). elaborate/read.wap:434-505 ranks them by fit and `nearest`. Until S3 a law can be Proved about another
  overload than the one that runs.
- **Proposal:** step 0's gate shows where the two choose differently. 0 core lines.

**R1.8 Effects against propositions** (not a split; keep).
- Only the theory has effects (elaborate/effects.wap, prelude/effects.wap); core/analyze has none.
- Refusing a computation where a value or a proof is wanted is the elaborator's own rule (plan 9.2, 9.6), the same in
  a compiler with one elaborator. S3 removes the asymmetry.

**R1.9 Annotations land on a phrase's first word** (not a split; a rule of the reader).
- **Now:** core/parse/cascade.wap:62 and 179-188 give an `@…` to the next item of a phrase:
  - `@x a + b` annotates `a`;
  - `@x enum E {…}` annotates the word `enum`, and the enum rule replaces that word, so the fact is lost.
- **Proposal:** an annotation that leads a phrase belongs to the whole phrase. About 5 lines in core/parse, which go
  to the interviewer as a core change.
- **Unblocks:** `@protocol`, `@final` and the others on any declaration form.

**What S3 as planned leaves open:**
- the order of S2 and S4, since the layouts come first;
- the three readings of a numeral;
- `int` running as the wrapping type, against the decision;
- layouts spelled in wap, which the branch has typed by the typer S3 deletes.

Steps 0 to 3 above close these before the switch.
